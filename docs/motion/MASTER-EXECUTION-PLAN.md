## The compounding library (Aaron's core philosophy, 2026-07-23)

One photo of a product in the store is a permanent seed asset, not a one-off input. Every seed fans out through the Gemini paths into a durable base: studio master (isolation/restage), alpha cutout (the item staged independently), placement renderings that blend the product into rooms, format variations, and the item's text details. That base then powers everything downstream: feed posts, micros, motion clips of the product being interacted with, films.

The point is compounding. Nothing here is throwaway; every layer is saved, named by slug, and re-runnable from manifests and recipes. When a new animation technique, skill, or model shows up (next month or next year), the instruction is: "use my existing library and go explore this new route" — one new capability runs across the ENTIRE base at once and multiplies every product photo ever taken. Motion clips are modular so they combine into bigger videos, and those bigger videos become library assets themselves. The library saves and supports itself; it is the foundation, and the foundation is the product.

Rules that keep this true (every agent, every session):
1. Never overwrite a master, cutout, placement, or clip — version alongside (`-v2`, `upscaled/`), keep the original.
2. One slug per product, used consistently across every folder and manifest, so a library-wide re-run is a loop, not archaeology.
3. Every new deliverable class gets its recipe logged in `PRODUCTION-RECIPES.md` the day it ships, so it can be re-run across the whole base later.
4. Text is never baked into pixels (locked 2026-07-22); scenes stay clean so words, languages, and offers can change forever for free.
5. When adopting any new tool or model, the first question is: "what does running this across the existing library produce?" Batch it across the base, not just the new item.

## Non-negotiable standards (Aaron has repeated these; do not re-learn them the hard way)

1. Motion graphics means product-cutout heroes in designed space (Apple / Kohler Shorts / Buff Motion grammar). NEVER camera pans over warehouse photos.
2. No AI people. No fake UGC. Ever.
3. Type: sans 300/700 weight stack, italic lead then bold name then skinny tracked line, centered margins, bold-on-void floor 120px at 1080 wide. Minimal words; never caption what the photo shows.
3b. Design language (Aaron, 2026-07-22): foundation is black/white/crisp/clear with the approved thin+bold sans blend. That is the spine, not a cage: within a piece, transitions and accent moments CAN go bold and colorful like the Buff Motion showreel, as long as it blends into one great whole and never reads vibe-coded (no random pills, boxes, gradients, or stock-template chrome). Every frame should look deliberately designed.
3c. TYPE CALIBRATION (Aaron, 2026-07-22 evening, vs Kohler shorts screenshots): heavy uppercase slabs + color bands + brand chips on product cards read "AI". Product/scene creatives use Kohler-quiet grammar: scene carries the color, one floating light-300 sentence-case stack, bold on a single word only, small tracked caps tag (see `PL-feed-post-v2`). Poster-bold uppercase is reserved for void punch cards only (FOR LESS / PAY LESS THAN BIG-BOX). Existing v1 feed posts and micro scenes B/C should be requieted before wide use.
4. Master format 9:16 mobile-first. Wide plates get staggered between two other images, never full-bleed.
5. Scenes 1-2s max. A 10s short is 4-5+ beats.
6. Facts (price, dims, savings) live ONLY in Remotion type, never in generated pixels. Never claim 40% off.
7. VERIFY every render visually before telling Aaron it's done. Encode yuv420p BT.709 limited for Mac. Show batches as contact sheets (montage on cream #f5f0e6).
7a-MOTION VOCABULARY LAW (Aaron, 2026-07-23 ~4:30 AM: BC clips "terribly unstable... wobbling... super slow with pauses" — REGENERATED): kling renders "dolly" as a person WALKING (bob + footsteps) and "slow" as near-static with lurching pauses. BANNED words in camera prompts: dolly, walkthrough, walk, slow. REQUIRED grammar for any camera move: "Camera glides [direction] on a motorized gimbal slider at a steady constant speed, perfectly stabilized, buttery smooth, no handheld shake, no walking bob, no footsteps, no pauses, continuous motion from first frame to last." Aaron wants gimbal-quality stability, always. Post-processing cannot rescue walking bob or pauses; the motion must be generated right. Test one or two clips with any new motion grammar BEFORE batching (Aaron's small-stages rule applies to generation too).
ITERATION 2-5 RESULT (Aaron, 2026-07-23): FIVE prompt grammars (dolly, gimbal rig, cinematic tour, commercial preview, pan-in) ALL produced walking bob on the aisle shot — words cannot stop kling from walking a travel shot. VERDICT (Aaron): the RAIL METHOD WINS. CAMERA RAIL LAW, locked: every camera-travel clip is generated with `--start-image <full photo>` + `--end-image <center-crop ~74% of the same photo, resized back to source dims>` and the minimal prompt "A smooth steady cinematic move from the first frame to the last frame, perfectly stable, seamless continuous motion at constant speed, no pauses. Keep everything exactly as it is. No people." (plus any real action like fan spin or pendant glow). Never prompt-describe camera travel again. Remotion AmbientPush (`AMB-push-*`) is the free fallback for simple pushes. Post-chain unchanged: bytedance upscale -> minterpolate 60 (no deshake needed on rails) -> Mac-safe encode -> frame gate -> Aaron playback gate.
7b. TEMPORAL GATE (learned 2026-07-23 ~3 AM, Aaron: "insanely choppy... having a seizure"): still-frame contact sheets are BLIND to judder and shake. Raw kling clips are 24fps with big per-frame motion (strobing) and fake-handheld wobble on camera moves. EVERY clip goes through the smooth pipeline before shipping: `deshake=rx=32:ry=32` (camera-move clips ONLY, never locked-camera clips) then `minterpolate=fps=60:mi_mode=mci:mc_mode=aobmc:vsbmc=1` (all clips), pattern in `smooth-library.sh`. NEVER mix frame rates down the chain: comps consume 60fps sources at 30fps (clean 2:1); rendering a 30fps comp over raw 24fps clips duplicates every 4th frame and stutters. Final playback judgment on actual motion belongs to Aaron until we have a better gate.
8. Show work as it completes. Small batches, visible progress.
9. RUN GENERATION IN PARALLEL (Aaron, 2026-07-23). Higgsfield jobs execute server-side; never submit them one-at-a-time and wait. Batch scripts launch workers in parallel waves (~5 concurrent), and each worker pipelines its own chain (generate -> download -> upscale) so no stage idles. Serial batch scripts are retired; pattern lives in `batch3-run.sh` style: jobs TSV + worker function + wave loop. Local verification/encode can proceed on finished files while later waves still generate. TWO HARD-WON GOTCHAS: (a) background workers inside a `while read` loop MUST launch with `< /dev/null` or a subprocess will eat the remaining jobs-file lines and silently skip jobs; (b) placement scenes live at `docs/motion/renders/placements/`, NOT `renders/placements/` — a wrong path fails instantly with no credit spent, logged as FAIL (gen).
